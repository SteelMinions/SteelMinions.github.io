About Us

Steel Minions supports students who are developing and releasing commercial software. While many projects are games, the studio also supports wider software development for research, clients, and student‑led ventures.

Students work with Steel Minions in a variety of ways:

  • Developing commercial software — often games, but not exclusively.
  • Working with external clients on real commercial briefs.
  • Supporting research projects that require bespoke software.
  • Developing student‑led projects intended for commercial release.
  • Forming small teams to run a studio as part of their placement year.

Steel Minions provides:

  • A dedicated workspace for development.
  • Access to specialist equipment and software.
  • Mentoring in development, testing, marketing, and publishing.

The studio is led by Dr. Mark Featherstone and supported by Luke Melville and Andrew Hamilton — all lecturers within the Games and Artificial Intelligence subject group.

Course Links

Students interact with Steel Minions in several ways throughout their studies:

  • Students come from a range of courses, most commonly Games Programming, Games Art, and Game Design.
  • Steel Minions provides commercial project ideas and feedback for taught modules.
  • Students can develop module‑based work into commercial projects through the studio.
  • Entrepreneurial placement students can work with Steel Minions for mentoring, workspace, and equipment.
  • Students preparing for competitions (e.g., Games Republic Showcase) can use studio facilities.
  • Recent graduates can continue developing commercial projects with access to studio support.

Finances

Steel Minions doesn’t usually take payment or share income, except where legally negotiating with publishers/clients or where unusual costs are involved (e.g., purchasing hardware/software). In such cases, Steel Minions releases the software, deducts costs, and passes the remainder to students. Steel Minions cannot finance projects; it provides support, mentoring, equipment, and workspace. Students must be paid at least minimum wage unless the work is part of their course.

In many cases, software is wholly owned by the students. The main requirement is that all commercial software released with Steel Minions support must carry a Steel Minions logo.
