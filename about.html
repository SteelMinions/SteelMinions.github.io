About Us

Who we are

Steel Minions is part of Sheffield Hallam University's School of Computing. It enables students and staff to work on games-related commercial or research projects. Students undertake the work, which is supervised by lecturers.

What we do

What we do Enthusiastic, smart students from games programming, art, and design courses work on developing software for clients. The software shows a possible prototype solution for a challenging technical problem. We can deploy solutions to mobile devices, the web, PCs, Virtual Reality and PlayStations. This could be:

  • A video game that students want to release commercially.
  • A game-related research project for another part of the university or a different institution.
  • Educational or serious games software for a commercial partner.
  • Gamification for a commercial partner (taking some existing business process or software, creating a new application that uses principles from game design to make the software more compelling and fun without it being a complete game).

How it usually works

Students are recruited, a supervisor is assigned and the project begins:

  • The team studies the proposal and makes some initial suggestions.
  • Analysis and specification follow, talking to clients, mocking up possible solutions, creating a final specification and schedule.
  • Software development begins and takes up a lot of the project time.
  • Software testing, this can involve back and forth with the client as the software is tested for faults, but also for suitability, ease of use. This can be time consuming. At this point the software is a working prototype, so it might not be as complete and polished as a commercially developed application.
  • The client engages the services of a commercial partner, we help them take over, either for further development of features, final preparation for commercial release to the public or ongoing maintenance.

What we don't do

We are not a commercial software development partner; we are a university with student workers who have educational commitments and graduation dates, we cannot provide a complete commercial software service with ongoing maintenance and strict deadlines. We can provide a prototype solution which the client can then take to a 3rd party commercial software developer for further development to market and ongoing support.

  • Software is not built once, perfect and never modified. Software is a living product that needs adjusting, fixing and enhancing, with regular updates, once it starts being used in ernest by the public.
  • Students cannot meet commercial deadlines and pressures as their studies will always come first.
  • Working with the university should be seen as a way for both parties to give something back to the community and each other, work experience for the students, experimental new technology solutions for the client delivered at low cost. If you need a commercial product, crucial to your bottom line, delivered on a tight deadline, then this is not the right approach.

Finances

Steel Minions doesn’t usually take any payment or share in any income, apart from where Steel Minions is legally negotiating with publishers/clients or there are unusual costs involved, for example, the project required Steel Minions itself to purchase new hardware or software. If this was the case then the software would be released by Steel Minions itself, with costs removed from any income, the remainder then passed to the students. Steel Minions cannot finance projects or provide financial support to students, it only provides support, advice, mentoring, access to certain equipment and office space. It is not run to make a profit. Students must be paid at least minimum wage unless the work is part of their course, SHU may also charge for staff time supervising projects.

In many cases the software is wholly owned by the students and Steel Minions isn’t involved in any payments. Sometimes the client may ask the students to sign over rights to software to the client. The main and often only requirement is that all commercial software released with the support of Steel Minions must carry a Steel Minions logo (alongside any other logos used within the software).
